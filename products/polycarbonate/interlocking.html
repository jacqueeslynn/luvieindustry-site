Set out the seven-wall facade

Begin with an elevation drawing that locates the seven-wall interlocking panels relative to openings, corners and the supporting frame. Confirm the installed module width from the actual connection detail rather than the loose panel width. Compare alternative starting positions to avoid unresolved narrow pieces at the final edge. Review a sample assembly to understand the visible rhythm of the ribs and joints.

Resolve perimeter and opening details

Ask for the proposed head, sill, jamb, corner and termination sections, including any edge profiles, seals and fixing components used in the system. Clarify how cut panels are retained and how interfaces accommodate movement and manage water. Match every perimeter detail to the elevation so that special pieces and accessories appear in the procurement schedule instead of being left for site improvisation.

Prepare the engineering review package

Collect the panel section, connection drawings, support assumptions, fixing schedule and relevant model-specific technical documents in one revision-controlled package. The project engineer should review the proposed assembly against the actual building conditions and local requirements. Ask the supplier to identify the scope and limitations of any test evidence. Before ordering, approve the panel layout, accessory quantities and installation instructions together; the seven-wall description alone is not a complete facade specification.

Build your project specification

  1. Application and destination: roof, facade, partition or glazing; project location and required approvals.
  2. Panel schedule: section type, thickness, cut length, effective width, quantity, colour and transparency.
  3. System details: support spacing, joints, fixings, end closures, drainage and movement allowances for engineering review.
  4. Evidence and order terms: request a current drawing, sample, model-specific test documents, packing plan and written quotation.
