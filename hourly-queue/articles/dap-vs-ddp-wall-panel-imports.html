Should a wall panel importer use DAP or DDP?

Independent evidence

ICC Incoterms 2020 explanation: The ICC explains the key distinction: under DAP the buyer handles import formalities, while under DDP the seller takes on import clearance and duties. This source does not certify a Luvie product; request evidence for the exact SKU.

An importer comparing “delivered” panel prices can easily miss who is responsible for customs clearance and taxes. DAP and DDP both name a destination, but the division of import obligations differs. The wrong assumption can delay a shipment even when the goods and freight price are agreed.

Short answer

Use DAP when the buyer will handle import clearance, duties and taxes at the named destination. Consider DDP only when the seller can legally and operationally perform the destination import obligations. Write the exact named place, unloading and document responsibilities into the contract.

Name the place precisely

“DAP Uzbekistan” or “DDP Brazil” is too vague for a usable quotation. Identify the address or terminal, delivery point, currency and whether local transport beyond the port is included. Under DAP, the seller bears the agreed transport to the named destination while the buyer handles import formalities. Under DDP, the seller assumes those import obligations. ICC’s Incoterms material is the primary reference for this allocation.

Test feasibility before offering DDP

A foreign supplier may not be able to act as importer of record or pay local duties in the destination country. Ask a licensed customs broker about the exact product classification, required documents and tax treatment before accepting a DDP price. Incoterms do not determine the tariff rate, transfer of title or every contract term. A “door-to-door” promise is not a substitute for a legally workable clearance path.

Model the full cost and handoff

Compare quotes using the same product quantities, cartons and destination. Write who supplies commercial invoice, packing list, transport documents and any test or origin paperwork. Confirm who pays unloading and who reports delivery damage. For a mixed container, separate panel families so classification and document questions do not disappear inside one blended line item.

Illustrative buyer situation

An Uzbekistan buyer asks for one price delivered to its warehouse. The supplier offers DDP, but neither party has confirmed who can act as importer of record. Before accepting the attractive all-in figure, the buyer should involve its customs broker, identify the product classification and compare a precisely named DAP warehouse quotation. The order should say who handles clearance, tax, unloading and damage evidence; “door to door” alone leaves too much unresolved.

Buyer checklist before ordering

  • Exact named delivery point and term version
  • Importer-of-record and customs broker
  • Duty, tax, unloading and local handling allocation
  • Product classification and document list

Save the approved sample, product code and document references with the purchase order. A catalog helps shortlist a finish but does not establish the final specification, local acceptance, price or delivery terms.

Common buyer questions

Does DDP mean the buyer has no paperwork?

Not necessarily. The seller handles import obligations under the term, but the parties must still coordinate destination documents and lawful clearance.

Is DAP the same as delivered duty paid?

No. Under DAP the buyer handles import clearance and duties.

Can Incoterms tell me the tariff?

No. Classification and destination customs rules determine duties and must be checked locally.
