Brazil PVC wall panels: reconcile a picking error without hiding the stock movement

A Brazilian distributor receives a photograph showing that a customer ordered one PVC finish but received another. Sending the correct cartons may solve the immediate complaint, yet leave two incorrect stock balances and an unexplained invoice adjustment. A picking error needs a linked movement record, not simply an apology and a manual change to the inventory total. This guide proposes an internal reconciliation process for a commercial misdispatch; it does not assume a manufacturing defect or define local legal remedies.

Short answer

Compare what was ordered, picked, dispatched and received using the original references and units. Control any replacement and return as separate linked movements, preserve the original transaction, and close the case only when physical quantities and authorized commercial adjustments agree. Do not make an unexplained stock adjustment to force the system to match.

Establish which mismatch actually occurred

Ask for the order reference and readable photographs of the received carton labels, avoiding unnecessary customer personal data. Compare the approved order version with the pick ticket, dispatch record and customer evidence. Distinguish a wrong finish from a wrong quantity, a mislabeled carton or an unauthorized order substitution. If the contents and label disagree, record both observations and seek further inspection rather than assuming the picker caused it. State quantities in panels and cartons with the confirmed conversion between them. Preserve original scans and timestamps. Identify the smallest defensible set of related movements for review, while keeping uncertain items visible instead of declaring a cause prematurely.

Check the other side of the warehouse balance

Inspect the intended stock location and the location of the item apparently sent. A wrong pick can create a shortage in one reference and an apparent surplus in another. Check adjacent orders if the evidence suggests cartons may have been exchanged, but do not assume every nearby shipment is affected. GS1 describes traceability as connecting product data and recorded events during the physical flow of goods. That principle supports reconstruction of the movement; it does not automatically prove who made an error. Record counted quantities, counting time and any movements occurring during the count so later dispatches do not make the comparison misleading.

Link the remedy to separately identified movements

Agree the proposed remedy with the responsible account owner and customer under the applicable commercial process. If replacement cartons are authorized, create a traceable replacement instruction that references the original order and incident. Do not rewrite the original dispatch as though the correct goods had always been sent. If goods will return, identify the expected reference, quantity and return authorization separately. On receipt, inspect condition before restoring stock availability; a customer return is not automatically saleable. Ask the finance team to handle any credit, charge or corrected document through its authorized procedures. Keep logistics events and financial decisions linked but do not treat one as proof that the other is complete.

Close the incident with a reproducible reconciliation

Prepare a short closing table showing original dispatch, replacement dispatch, actual return, disposition and approved adjustments by reference and unit. Explain any quantity that remains outstanding rather than hiding it in a general variance. Have someone other than the original adjustment author review the evidence where practical. Test whether the remaining physical stock agrees with the recorded balance after the documented movements. Then address the demonstrated cause: clearer location labels, stronger reference confirmation or a controlled order-change handoff may be appropriate. Avoid adding burdensome checks unrelated to the evidence. Retain the incident link so a later return, invoice question or repeated complaint can be understood without reconstructing the case from messages.

Before you request a quotation

  • Approved order and actual carton identities compared.
  • Both intended and incorrectly shipped stock locations reconciled.
  • Replacement, return and financial decisions recorded separately.
  • Closing quantities and any remaining discrepancy independently reviewed.

Frequently asked questions

Should we delete the incorrect dispatch and enter the replacement?

Preserve the original history. Record the correction through the authorized workflow with linked replacement or return movements so the physical sequence remains understandable.

Can returned cartons go straight back into available stock?

Only after the applicable receiving and condition checks. Record the reference, quantity and disposition rather than assuming unopened-looking cartons are automatically suitable for resale.
