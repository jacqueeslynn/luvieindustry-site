PVC panels for Brazil: agree the sample plan before destructive testing

A small decorative sample may help a Brazilian distributor choose a finish, but it may not be a suitable specimen for the laboratory question that follows. If a test consumes the material, an incorrect cut or missing identifier can leave the buyer with a report that cannot be connected confidently to the proposed order. Before booking shipment or asking a supplier to prepare pieces, separate the testing purpose from the colour-approval process. This is a procurement planning guide, not a test method or a statement that any Luvie model has passed testing.

Short answer

Ask the selected laboratory to define specimen preparation, quantity, identification and handling for the requested method. Agree who selects the source material and what population the sample is intended to represent. Keep a documented link from the original product to the specimens and arrange retention or disposal before destructive work begins. Do not infer approval of a whole product range from one submitted sample.

Write the question the test must answer

Identify the exact PVC reference and the property or project requirement being investigated. Ask the laboratory to confirm a suitable method and the limits of the resulting report; do not choose a method simply because its title appears in a competitor's brochure. Intertek's building-product testing guidance explains that sample needs depend on the test method and whether testing is destructive. This supports asking for a laboratory-specific plan, not copying a universal sample size. The cited guidance concerns testing services and does not certify Luvie or establish Brazilian project acceptance. Have the responsible project adviser confirm which evidence is actually needed before spending money on a test that answers a different question.

Decide who selects and prepares the specimens

Record whether the supplier, buyer, independent inspector or laboratory selects the source panels. Describe the available lot or production reference and ask the laboratory how that selection affects any claim about representativeness. A hand-picked showroom piece should not silently become evidence for an entire shipment. Obtain written instructions about cutting, conditioning, orientation, surface treatment and any assembly components required by the chosen method. Ask who performs preparation and how preparation is recorded. Do not wash, sand, remove a finish or add an adhesive merely to make a specimen look tidy. If the laboratory needs an installed assembly rather than a bare panel, resolve that distinction before packaging.

Keep identity continuous through packing and receipt

Assign identifiers that connect each submitted piece to the source panel and accompanying request. Photograph the material and labels before packing, while following the laboratory's directions on where marks may be placed without interfering with the test. List pieces separately from cartons and identify any retained companion material without implying that it is identical in every relevant respect. Record who handed the parcel over, its tracking reference and the laboratory's receipt acknowledgement. Ask the laboratory to report damaged, mismatched or insufficient specimens before proceeding. A courier delivery notification establishes arrival, not acceptance of the sample condition or suitability for the planned method.

Agree what happens after the specimen is consumed

Before work starts, decide whether remaining material is retained, returned or disposed of, and who authorizes additional testing. Ask about any practical limits or charges rather than assuming indefinite storage. When the report arrives, compare its sample description and identifiers with the submission record and check any stated deviations. Route technical interpretation to the responsible qualified reviewer. Keep the tested configuration distinct from later changes in product reference, finish or assembly. If a purchase decision relies on the report, document the precise scope of that decision. A passed result for submitted specimens is not automatically a batch-release decision, a market certificate or a guarantee for every future order.

Before you request a quotation

  • Testing question and intended method agreed with the laboratory.
  • Selection and preparation responsibilities documented.
  • Source, specimen and receipt identifiers connected.
  • Retention, further testing and report-review authority assigned.

Frequently asked questions

Can the colour sample also be the laboratory specimen?

Only if the laboratory confirms that its identity, dimensions, condition and preparation suit the requested method. Visual approval alone does not establish that suitability.

Does retaining another piece guarantee a valid retest?

No. Ask the laboratory whether the retained material and its storage are suitable for the intended follow-up. A companion piece does not automatically reproduce the original test configuration.

Next step
