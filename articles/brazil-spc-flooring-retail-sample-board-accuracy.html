SPC Sample Boards in Brazil: Avoid Misleading Displays

A compact sample board helps a Brazilian flooring retailer sell from limited showroom space. It can also create avoidable complaints when the display hides pattern variation, changes scale or mixes a discontinued sample with a current quotation. A useful SPC display should support a clear buyer decision, not simply make every finish look uniform.

Short answer

Keep display, quotation and stock identity connected. Show enough material to discuss pattern repetition and joints, and keep technical claims in a separate, version-controlled document.

Independent reference

Choose a display format that answers a buying question

A small chip is useful for comparing broad colours but cannot represent a complete room. Combine a portable colour board with a larger assembled panel or loose planks when possible. Explain what each display is intended to show. For a retailer serving apartments in Brazil, a practical conversation includes furniture colour, lighting and the transition to adjacent rooms. Do not use a room rendering as proof of the exact delivered pattern. Identify illustrations as illustrations and compare the chosen finish with physical material before approval.

Prevent the display from becoming a different product

Give each sample an internal identifier linked to the supplier item code and revision. Record when the sample arrived and which specification accompanies it. If the supplier changes a surface, attached backing or joint system, review the display instead of simply keeping the familiar commercial name. Store a reference photograph of the board and a separate photograph of the label on the actual sample. This simple distinction matters when a sales representative shows an older mobile image while the warehouse holds a newer version.

Discuss variation without inventing a tolerance

Ask for several planks from the proposed range to understand the available visual variation, then agree how the buyer will evaluate the appearance. Do not promise that every plank will match a single chip. Conversely, do not use natural variation as a blanket excuse for a different product. Separate intentional pattern variation from damage and printing defects in the written discussion. An ISO quality-management certificate does not establish what this specific floor must look like; the approved reference and order documents must carry that information.

Give staff a repeatable handover routine

Place a current technical-sheet link or document reference beside the display, including the language the local sales team understands. Remove unsupported badges such as scratch-proof or suitable everywhere. Train staff to record the chosen finish, room use and pending installer checks before requesting a quotation. When an order is received, compare its labels with the display record before replenishing samples. Review slow-moving and withdrawn displays periodically so customers are not sold an appearance that cannot be confirmed for their order.

Before you request a quotation

  • Identify each board and its supplier product code.
  • Use multiple planks to discuss visual variation.
  • Separate illustrative room images from sample evidence.
  • Retire displays when the underlying specification changes.

Frequently asked questions

Can a phone photograph replace a sample?

It is useful for shortlisting, but lighting and screens can alter appearance. Approve important finishes using physical material.

Should all technical claims go on the sample board?

Keep the board readable and link to a controlled specification. Only display claims supported for that exact product.
