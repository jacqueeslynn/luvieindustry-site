SPC Flooring in Saudi Hotels: Room Handover Planning

A hotel refurbishment can finish one room while adjoining rooms remain in use. For a Saudi project buying SPC flooring, the operational handover deserves its own plan. A room that looks complete may still contain unresolved thresholds, cleaning questions or undocumented replacement material. Procurement should connect the selected flooring to a room-by-room release process rather than assume that the delivery date is the same as the date a guest can use the space.

Short answer

Define the conditions for releasing each room, the responsible signatories and the product information that housekeeping needs. Keep installation completion, cleaning acceptance and operational reopening as separate decisions.

Define the room release record

Give each room or agreed zone a reference that can be linked to the flooring batch and installation record. Record the finish code, installation date, outstanding items and the person authorised to release it. In a phased Saudi hotel project, housekeeping, engineering and the contractor may inspect different things. Agree these responsibilities before work starts so an attractive photograph is not mistaken for complete acceptance. Any required waiting periods or environmental conditions must come from the actual product and installation instructions, not a generic rule copied from another flooring system.

Sequence work around the floor rather than across it

Ask which trades still need to enter after the floor is installed. Furniture assembly, curtain installation and maintenance work can affect the finished surface and should be coordinated. Specify approved temporary protection and removal responsibility without assuming any covering is compatible. Do not tape directly to a surface unless the product guidance permits it. The Tarkett care reference demonstrates that movement and protection instructions can be product-specific; it is not a substitute for the selected SPC instructions. The project team should review both the finishing sequence and the route for moving equipment into the room.

Give housekeeping an actual care package

Hand over the supplier-approved cleaning information with the product reference and identify any products or methods that remain unconfirmed. A housekeeping team needs a usable instruction, not a claim that the floor is waterproof and therefore accepts any process. Show where grit can accumulate at entries and how furniture feet should be inspected under the applicable guidance. Clarify the route for reporting a mark or damaged edge before an improvised repair is attempted. If multilingual instructions are required, keep the approved original and the translation together so technical meaning can be checked.

Link spare material to rooms and future work

Record the location and identity of retained replacement material, including the finish and batch information that is available. Do not promise an invisible repair or future availability of an identical batch. Ask the installer what a local replacement entails for the actual locking system and room layout. Before the room is released, review transitions, visible edges and any remaining protection with the responsible team. A signed room record makes later discussions more precise: it distinguishes an installation item from damage caused after handover without prejudging liability or reducing the need to investigate the evidence.

Before you request a quotation

  • Create one release record per room or agreed zone.
  • Coordinate remaining trades and approved temporary protection.
  • Provide housekeeping with model-specific care instructions.
  • Label replacement material and record where it is stored.

Frequently asked questions

Does completion of installation mean immediate reopening?

Not necessarily. Follow product instructions and the project release process, including cleaning and outstanding-work checks.

Can retained spare boards guarantee an invisible future repair?

No. Appearance, ageing, batch variation and the replacement method all require review.
