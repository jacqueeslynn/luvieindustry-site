Return unused PU panel cartons to stock carefully

Unused PU stone panel cartons in Uzbekistan should not return to saleable stock simply because a project says they were never installed. Packaging may have been opened, pieces may have been mixed, and the site may have exposed them to unsuitable conditions. A distributor needs a receiving decision that protects the next buyer without assuming every returned carton is defective.

Short answer

Agree return eligibility commercially, then inspect identity, count, condition and packaging before assigning a stock status. Keep accepted intact goods, opened goods and items awaiting review separate. A warehouse inspection does not automatically create a supplier refund entitlement.

Confirm the commercial agreement first

Identify the original order and ask what the customer is proposing to return. Check whether the agreed terms cover unopened surplus, opened cartons or custom pieces. Do not promise a credit before this scope is resolved. Record whether the request concerns unused surplus or an alleged product problem; they need different supporting information. The supplier and distributor may have different contracts, so a dealer’s return promise should not be presented as the supplier’s policy.

Verify model and quantity without guessing

Compare carton labels with the original supply record and count the actual pieces where the carton has been opened. Check whether finishes or batches were mixed during the project. A familiar texture does not prove identity. Keep uncertain pieces apart until the supplier can help identify them. If a label is missing, record that fact rather than recreating an original production label from memory or an unrelated carton.

Inspect faces, edges and storage history

Ask where the cartons were kept and whether they were exposed to water, construction dust, direct sunlight or other site conditions. Review the selected product’s storage guidance. Inspect the visible faces and edges without assuming that an intact-looking outer box guarantees undamaged contents. Photograph any concern and retain the original packaging during review. Avoid aggressive cleaning or touch-up work that could hide the condition in which the goods were returned.

Assign an honest stock status

Accepted intact cartons can follow the agreed stock procedure; opened or incomplete goods need a distinct record and an accurate description if they are later offered for sale. Items awaiting investigation should not be mixed with normal picking stock. HSE handling guidance is a useful general reference for safely moving bulky packages, not an acceptance standard for PU panels. Have staff use the actual package information and site handling assessment.

Close the record and preserve traceability

Document the accepted count, rejected or held items, photographs and the agreed commercial outcome. If packaging is replaced, retain the original model and batch record without claiming the new carton is factory-sealed. Update both physical stock and the sales system before another order is picked. The next buyer should receive the condition and quantity promised, not an undisclosed collection of project leftovers assembled into an apparently complete carton.

Before you request a quotation

  • Resolve eligibility before promising credit.
  • Verify identity and actual piece count.
  • Separate intact, opened and held stock.
  • Retain condition records and original traceability.

Frequently asked questions

Does unused mean suitable for resale?

Not automatically. Identity, condition, storage history and packaging still need review, and any later sales description must be accurate.

Does accepting a customer return guarantee supplier reimbursement?

No. The commercial agreements are separate. Confirm the relevant terms and evidence with the supplier rather than assuming a matching entitlement.
