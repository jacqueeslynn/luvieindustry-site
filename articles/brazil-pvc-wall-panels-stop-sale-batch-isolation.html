Brazil PVC wall panels: isolate a suspect batch before another order leaves

A batch reference is useful only if the warehouse can act on it. When a Brazilian distributor receives a credible complaint about PVC wall panels, the immediate operational question is not how attractive the tracking spreadsheet looks. It is which cartons can still leave the building, which orders already contain the same stock and who can authorize the next movement. This guide describes a proposed internal control workflow, not a claim that a particular product has a defect or a substitute for applicable Brazilian obligations.

Short answer

Place potentially affected stock on a documented sales and dispatch hold, connect physical cartons to inventory and order records, and preserve the evidence needed to decide the scope. Keep investigation, customer communication and release authority separate. A supplier explanation alone should not silently remove a warehouse hold.

Define the hold without guessing the cause

Record the complaint date, supplier reference, finish, carton identifiers, purchase order and the condition reported. Describe observations rather than declaring a technical cause before examination. If a carton has no usable batch marking, identify it by its receiving record and location and explicitly record the uncertainty. Start with the defensible scope available from the evidence; if records cannot separate suspect stock from other deliveries, a broader temporary hold may be needed. Assign an incident owner and an alternate so the decision does not disappear when one employee is absent. Preserve photographs and original messages without editing away useful context.

Make the physical and digital blocks agree

Move cartons into a clearly identified hold location where practical and prevent picking through the inventory process. A paper label is not sufficient if the online channel can still allocate the same stock. Review showroom samples, opened cartons, returns and stock in third-party storage. Ask each location to acknowledge the instruction and report its quantity in the same unit. Separate panels, cartons and square metres rather than adding unlike numbers. Record any movement made for inspection so examined material cannot accidentally return to available inventory. A named warehouse owner should verify the block, not merely assume that an email was read.

Reconcile orders before communicating a conclusion

Build a movement table showing received quantities, remaining stock, dispatched orders, returns and any documented losses or samples. Investigate unexplained differences before narrowing the hold. GS1 traceability guidance provides a useful framework for connecting identifiers and supply-chain events, but an identifier itself does not investigate a complaint or determine legal responsibilities. Identify which customers may have received relevant stock and preserve the order links. Customer-facing wording should distinguish a precautionary check from a confirmed defect. Consult the responsible importer and appropriate advisers about required notifications and actions; this workflow does not establish Brazilian recall thresholds.

Release only against a recorded disposition

Write down what evidence supports release, replacement, further inspection or another disposition, who approved it and which exact units it covers. Keep retained samples and inspection records linked to the incident where appropriate. Releasing one verified portion must not automatically release every carton sharing a finish name. Update both the warehouse status and sales channels, then check a test order against the intended available quantity. Close with a short review of why the stock could or could not be isolated quickly. Improve receiving labels, unit definitions or order links where the investigation revealed gaps, while preserving the original incident history.

Before you request a quotation

  • Incident owner, observations and provisional batch scope recorded.
  • Warehouse, online allocation and third-party stock holds reconciled.
  • Receipts, dispatches, returns and remaining quantities compared.
  • Disposition evidence and exact release authority retained.

Frequently asked questions

Does a stop-sale prove the panels are defective?

No. A precautionary hold controls movement while the facts are examined. Explain that distinction and avoid making an unsupported technical or legal conclusion.

Can the sales team remove the hold after a supplier phone call?

Use a documented decision by the designated authority, supported by the relevant evidence. Record the conversation, but do not let an informal reassurance bypass the agreed process.
