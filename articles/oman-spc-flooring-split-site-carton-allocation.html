SPC Flooring in Oman: Splitting One Order Across Sites

An Oman distributor may buy one SPC shipment for several contractors or branches. The saving in purchasing effort can disappear if cartons are allocated casually and one site receives the wrong finish or incomplete accessory set. Splitting an order is therefore an allocation problem before it is a transport problem. The stock record should connect each delivery to the approved product and the quantity basis used by that site.

Short answer

Allocate cartons and accessories against named site requirements before dispatch, preserve product identity and record every transfer. Do not treat similar finishes or spare cartons from another project as interchangeable without confirmation.

Freeze the destination requirements

For each site, record the finish reference, approved quantity, accessory needs and the person authorised to accept delivery. Keep measured area separate from ordered quantity so the reason for any allowance remains visible. One contractor may need a doorway trim that another does not, even when both buy the same flooring. Ask for the current layout where quantities remain uncertain. Do not use a blanket allowance to conceal missing measurements. The allocation plan should show pending decisions before the warehouse begins picking, especially where several Oman sites request delivery during the same period.

Reserve identifiable stock rather than an abstract area

Link each reservation to the product and available carton or batch information. Confirm the actual coverage per carton from the supplied specification instead of assuming that all SPC cartons cover the same area. Where appearance continuity matters, ask the supplier and installer how the available stock should be managed. Do not promise that different batches will match perfectly. Separate damaged or inspection-held cartons from usable allocated stock so an apparent warehouse balance does not overstate what is ready to ship. Keep accessories under the same destination reference rather than in an unassigned pile.

Control changes after picking starts

If one site needs extra material, record the approved reallocation and check the effect on every affected destination. A quick verbal instruction can leave another contractor short and erase the reason for the discrepancy. Keep the original reservation, revised quantity and authorising person in the stock record. When loose planks or opened cartons are transferred, preserve the original product identity and condition information. HSE handling guidance supports assessing safe movement and access; it does not establish carton stacking limits. Use the supplier packaging guidance and suitable equipment for the actual loading task.

Close the delivery loop with the receiving team

Send a destination-specific packing list and ask the receiving person to check identity and visible condition against it. Record discrepancies promptly and distinguish shortage, wrong item and damage rather than calling everything a quality problem. Retain the dispatch and receiving records together. If material comes back from a site, inspect and identify it before returning it to available stock. Do not assume opened returns can be sold as an untouched full carton. A clear allocation history supports investigation and future ordering without promising that any particular claim will be accepted or that replacement stock is immediately available.

Before you request a quotation

  • Record each site’s approved product, quantity and accessories.
  • Reserve identifiable cartons using verified coverage information.
  • Authorise and document reallocations before dispatch.
  • Match receiving records and inspect returned material.

Frequently asked questions

Can spare cartons from another site be substituted automatically?

No. Confirm product identity, condition and any appearance or batch implications first.

Is total square-metre stock enough for allocation?

No. The available cartons, coverage, condition and accessories also need to match each site.
