PU stone panels in Uzbekistan: approve the wall under its intended lighting

A PU stone-look sample viewed on a desk can look quite different once light skims down a finished feature wall. In an Uzbekistan showroom or reception project, buyers should approve the relationship between texture, joints and lighting—not only a small color chip. A practical mockup helps distinguish the chosen relief from an installation defect before a larger order is released.

Short answer

Review a representative joined-panel mockup with the proposed light position and normal viewing conditions. Record the panel model, orientation, joint treatment and lighting setup together. A rendering or phone image can support the discussion, but it cannot replace an agreed physical appearance reference or establish product performance.

Independent reference

ERCO: grazing light and surface texture. General guidance, not a Luvie product certification.

Decide whether texture or visual uniformity comes first

ERCO describes grazing light as illumination arriving at a shallow angle to emphasize surface texture. That principle explains why a deeply textured stone-look finish may become more pronounced near a light source. Decide with the designer whether that emphasis is the intention. For a Tashkent showroom display, compare a texture-led feature wall with the softer appearance required behind small merchandise. Do not assume that the same finish and lighting arrangement suit both locations simply because the panel color is identical.

Build a representative comparison, not an isolated sample

Include the selected panel orientation, at least one relevant joint and the proposed finish treatment in the sample assembly. Ask the designer and installer to choose a representative mockup size for the actual wall. Place the proposed luminaire as intended, subject to its manufacturer’s requirements and professional electrical coordination. This article gives no universal lamp distance, temperature limit or mounting detail for PU panels. Change one lighting variable at a time so the team can identify what caused a difference in appearance.

Inspect from the positions customers will actually use

Look from the entrance, the main seating or browsing position and a closer maintenance position agreed for the project. Check both normal daytime conditions and the intended evening lighting scene. A shadow beside a raised stone shape may be intentional, while an abrupt joint step may require installer review. Ask the team to distinguish those observations explicitly. Avoid approving a wall only from an extreme angle chosen to hide seams, or rejecting it solely under a temporary work light that will not remain in the space.

Freeze the approved combination before ordering

Keep the sample identifier, panel layout, joint method, luminaire details and photographs in one approval record. Record any limitations of the sample and who signs off the appearance. If the light position, finish or panel orientation changes later, review the combination again rather than assuming the previous approval transfers. For an Uzbekistan order serving several branches, circulate the same record to each local installer and identify where site conditions differ. Separate the visual decision from checks on substrate, fixing, fire requirements and other technical suitability.

Before you request a quotation

  • Uzbekistan project location, wall elevation and intended viewing positions.
  • Selected PU stone-look model, orientation and joined-panel sample.
  • Proposed luminaire information and recorded lighting conditions.
  • Signed appearance reference and a review rule for later changes.

Frequently asked questions

Can grazing light make panel joints more noticeable?

It can emphasize surface changes. Evaluate the actual panel and joint treatment in the proposed arrangement instead of assuming a joint will disappear.

Can a phone photograph be the only approval reference?

It is useful for identification and discussion, but exposure, white balance and the screen affect appearance. Retain the agreed physical reference and the conditions under which it was reviewed.
