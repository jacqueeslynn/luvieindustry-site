PVC wall panels in UAE interiors: assign custody of replacement stock

At the end of an interior project, unused wall panels can become somebody else's problem. The installer has finished, the purchasing team has closed the delivery record, and the facilities team may not know which cartons belong to its building. For a UAE importer supplying PVC wall panels, the useful procurement question is not simply how much spare material to buy. It is who will control that material after completion, and what information the next maintenance contractor will receive when a repair becomes necessary.

Short answer

Name a stock custodian before delivery. Separate usable replacement panels from damaged material and installation offcuts, record the exact product and storage location, and require an issue record whenever material leaves the store. Ask for model-specific storage and repair guidance; spare stock does not itself prove that an installed panel can be removed or replaced safely.

Make custody a purchasing decision

Put the intended recipient of replacement stock into the purchase and handover documents. The owner, operator and fit-out contractor may be different organizations, so a name such as building management is not enough. Identify a role, an accessible contact and the person authorized to accept the material. Agree whether the stock belongs to one tenant, one floor or the whole property. This avoids transferring reserved panels to another job without permission. If the property operator has not yet been appointed, specify an interim custodian and a later transfer procedure rather than leaving ownership unresolved.

Accept actual material, not an estimated balance

At handover, count complete panels and inspect their visible condition with the receiving party. List loose pieces and offcuts separately; do not count every leftover piece as a usable replacement. Record the offered model, finish reference, delivery reference and any available batch marking. Photograph the labels and material condition, and connect the record to the original approval. A spare panel can help compare appearance but cannot guarantee an invisible repair after the installed finish has aged. Keep packaging where useful for identification, without assuming that an unopened carton confirms its contents or condition.

Give the custodian usable storage instructions

Obtain instructions for the exact panel construction and packaging before choosing a storage position. Altro's installation guide, for its own wall sheets, describes flat supported storage and warns about distortion on uneven surfaces. This illustrates why storage geometry matters, but its conditions are not specifications for Luvie panels. Ask Luvie to confirm the relevant requirements for the offered model; do not copy another manufacturer's temperatures or conditioning periods into the contract. The custodian should know the approved location, how the panels are supported, what protects their faces and how staff can inspect them without repeatedly moving the entire stock. Record relocation when the store changes.

Control release and repair approval separately

Use a simple issue record with the date, quantity, destination and remaining balance. Require the person collecting material to identify the repair location and the authorized requester. The maintenance contractor must still review the installation system, adjacent services and removal method before starting work. Having a spare board does not establish that a bonded installation can be dismantled without damage or that a new adhesive is compatible. After the repair, record what was consumed and whether unused material returned in acceptable condition. If the remaining stock is low, request a current offer rather than assuming the original finish is still available or will match an aged surface.

Before you request a quotation

  • Named owner, custodian and authorized requester recorded.
  • Full panels, offcuts and damaged stock counted separately.
  • Exact model and applicable storage instructions filed.
  • Issue log and remaining-stock review responsibility agreed.

Frequently asked questions

Does every project need a fixed percentage of spare panels?

No universal percentage is established here. Agree the reserve with the project team using its repair strategy, storage capacity and confirmed packaging, rather than treating every leftover as planned stock.

Can the custodian authorize a repair just by releasing a panel?

Not necessarily. Material release and technical approval are different responsibilities. Identify who approves the repair method and checks any surrounding services before the maintenance contractor begins.
