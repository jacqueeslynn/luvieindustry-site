SPC flooring for Chile: review the robot mop program before purchasing

An apartment operator or retailer in Chile may plan to clean a new SPC floor with equipment bought by another team. The flooring enquiry then needs more than a statement that the robot works on hard floors. Vacuum-only operation, damp wiping and an automated wash station are different parts of a cleaning proposal. The purchasing task is to obtain a compatible, documented combination before promising an unattended routine. This guide does not approve a particular robot, detergent or Luvie flooring model, and it does not replace either manufacturer's current instructions.

Short answer

Identify the exact floor and robot, then compare both sets of instructions for cleaning mode, liquid, pads and maintenance. Ask the suppliers to resolve conflicts in writing. Review docking and service arrangements as well as the moving robot. Do not treat a machine-approved cleaner or a generic hard-floor claim as permission to use that cleaner and program on the selected SPC finish.

Describe the actual program the operator intends to run

Record the robot reference, planned mode, pad or brush type and any settings the operator expects to change. Distinguish dry collection from a program that applies liquid; do not assume that a successful vacuum demonstration approves wet cleaning. Include the intended areas, frequency and whether a person will supervise the first runs. Ask the equipment supplier for the current instructions rather than copying a short retailer description. If the robot has not been selected, mark automated wet cleaning as an open procurement question. This keeps the flooring decision from being tied to an unspecified appliance that arrives after installation.

Check the cleaning liquid from both directions

List the proposed liquid by its actual name and obtain its current instructions. iRobot's setup guidance identifies cleaning solutions allowed for the models it discusses; that is an equipment-side restriction, not approval for every flooring finish. Send the same proposed liquid and operating method to the flooring supplier for model-specific review. Do not substitute a disinfectant, fragrance or concentrated detergent merely because it fits in the tank. If the two suppliers' instructions do not align, keep the combination unapproved and ask for an alternative. Record any agreed preparation and use instructions without inventing a universal dilution, water setting or cleaning frequency for SPC products.

Include the dock and servicing area in the review

Show where the robot will dock and where tanks, pads or trays will be handled. Ask the equipment supplier how the proposed station should be installed and maintained, and ask the floor supplier to review the intended contact conditions. Do not assume a protective mat solves an unconfirmed arrangement; its backing and suitability also need review. Plan how an operator will identify and respond to a spill or equipment fault instead of leaving that responsibility undefined. Keep servicing away from unfinished installation work and follow the responsible installer's handover requirements. These are coordination questions, not a claim that a specific dock will leak or damage a floor.

Approve a bounded trial and preserve the settings

If the suppliers permit a trial, agree the location, responsible observer and conditions before starting. Record the floor reference, equipment model, pad, liquid and relevant settings so a later change is visible. Inspect and document observations according to the agreed plan; stop and seek advice if unexpected residue, surface change or standing liquid appears. A short demonstration is not a durability certificate or permission for every future software setting. At handover, provide the approved routine and a change-review contact to the cleaning team. Recheck substitutions in liquids, pads or equipment and material changes to the program rather than assuming the original decision covers them automatically.

Before you request a quotation

  • Floor, robot, cleaning mode and accessories identified.
  • Liquid reviewed against both manufacturers' instructions.
  • Dock location and servicing responsibility recorded.
  • Trial limits, operating settings and change review documented.

Frequently asked questions

Does hard-floor compatibility prove suitability for this SPC?

No. A broad equipment category does not identify the selected floor finish or the proposed wet-cleaning conditions. Request confirmation for the actual combination.

Can a successful short trial replace written instructions?

No. It can document observations within agreed conditions, but it does not establish long-term compatibility or override either manufacturer's restrictions.
