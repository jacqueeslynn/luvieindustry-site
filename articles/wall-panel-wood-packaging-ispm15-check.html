What should wall panel importers check about wood packaging?

A wall panel may be plastic-based while its pallet, crate or loose blocking is made from timber. The product description therefore does not answer the wood-packaging question. Importers buying for South America, the Gulf or Uzbekistan should resolve the actual packing construction with their supplier and destination adviser before shipment. This guide helps prepare that discussion; it is not a country-specific customs ruling or a promise that a particular consignment will be accepted.

Short answer

Check every packaging component, not just the panels or the crate face. Identify raw timber, processed-wood parts and loose dunnage, then ask the destination's plant-protection authority or qualified import adviser which current requirements apply. Do not assume that calling a crate plywood resolves a mixed-material package.

Independent reference

Australian Department of Agriculture: ISPM 15 export FAQ — confirm destination rules separately. General guidance, not a Luvie product certification.

Separate the product from the packing materials

Request a packing-material list alongside the product and carton specifications. Include pallets, skids, framing, cross-battens, internal blocks and loose supports. Photographs should show the assembled package and its construction, not only a wrapped exterior that conceals the base. The Australian agriculture authority's FAQ distinguishes raw-wood packaging from packaging made wholly of qualifying processed wood, and notes that requirements depend on importing countries. This is useful background, not a substitute for checking the destination. Do not infer treatment status from the panel's material or a supplier's generic export experience. A product order and its packing arrangement are separate things that both need clear descriptions.

Review mixed materials and any later substitutions

A plywood-sided crate can still include raw timber in its base or framework. Ask the packer to identify such components before approving the arrangement. If a forwarder adds support timber during consolidation, that addition also belongs in the review. Avoid approving only a drawing while allowing any locally available substitute at packing time. Instead, record the approved material construction and require notification of a change. Ask your destination adviser how to handle the actual mix and which evidence is needed. Do not treat the source FAQ's exemptions as universal permission for every shipment, or apply a mark yourself. Treatment and marking must follow the applicable authorised system, and the responsible provider should be identifiable.

Request evidence at a useful point in the shipment

Agree when photographs and packing details will be available for review, ideally before the finished units become inaccessible. Ask for clear images of applicable marks where required, their location on the package and the package identification to which they relate. A blurred photograph or a generic certificate detached from the actual packing is difficult to reconcile with the shipment. Send the evidence to the appointed import adviser or broker for the destination rather than asking a sales representative to guarantee customs acceptance. Also record the final package count and any changes after inspection. These are practical traceability requests; they do not establish that photographs alone meet the destination's documentary or inspection requirements.

Keep phytosanitary checks separate from transport protection

Wood-packaging compliance does not by itself demonstrate that panels are protected against bending, corner damage, water entry or shifting. Review mechanical protection and transport handling as a separate packing discussion. Likewise, strong wrapping does not settle plant-health requirements. Put two distinct acceptance lines into the order handoff: destination-related packaging review, and suitability of the agreed protective packing for the product and route. If a crate or pallet is repaired, reused or replaced, ask whether that changes the previous acceptance. Before dispatch, reconcile the approved construction, final photographs and shipment identifiers. Unresolved destination requirements should be raised before cargo moves rather than being concealed behind a broad export-ready label.

Next step

Send your destination, intended transport route and packing constraints with the product inquiry. Confirm current packaging requirements through the relevant destination authority or import professional before approving dispatch.
