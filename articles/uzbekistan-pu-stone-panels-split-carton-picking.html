PU stone panels in Uzbekistan: protect partial orders after opening the original carton

An intact import carton may become several smaller customer orders at an Uzbekistan distributor’s warehouse. Once opened, the original protective arrangement and quantity record no longer describe every outgoing package. Plan this handoff before selling panels individually. This guide focuses on picking and repacking after receipt, not international freight pricing or a claim that one carton design works for every PU stone-effect panel.

Short answer

Keep product and batch identity through the split, inspect the picked pieces, protect the remaining stock and approve packaging for the actual onward delivery. A smaller order still needs its own quantity and condition record.

Define the sale unit before staff open cartons

Confirm whether sales are recorded by panel, carton or another agreed unit, and link each to the current packing information. Mark the storage location and keep the original label with the remaining stock. When a carton serves several orders, record the picked and remaining quantities rather than relying on memory. For a Tashkent distributor supplying branches and installers, identify the destination order separately from the original import shipment. Do not merge similar-looking finishes without checking their references.

Inspect at the point of picking

Prepare a clean supported area and follow the supplier’s handling guidance for the actual panel. Check exposed edges and relief faces before repacking, and set uncertain pieces aside for review. Record existing marks rather than allowing them to become an unexplained dispute at delivery. Avoid dragging finished faces against each other. Confirm how the open balance is protected during storage; an opened carton is not equivalent to the original closed package.

Design the smaller package for the next journey

DHL’s packing advice recommends separating fragile items rather than allowing them to collide within a package. Use this general principle to discuss an appropriate packing method, not to assume that a parcel guide certifies a PU panel shipment. Ask the supplier or packing specialist how to support the selected profile and protect its edges and relief. Confirm the transport mode and handling conditions with the local carrier. Reused boxes and a fragile label alone do not establish adequate protection.

Reconcile stock and obtain a clear handoff

Check picked quantity against the order, record the new package count and preserve the product and batch link in the dispatch record. Tell the receiver what is inside without inventing a new supplier batch number. Keep photographs where useful and identify who approves repacking and releases the order. Review repeat damage by stage: storage, picking, packing or delivery. Correct the stage supported by evidence instead of automatically blaming either the imported product or the driver.

Before you request a quotation

  • Sale units linked to current packing data
  • Original identity retained with open stock
  • Inspection and approved onward packaging
  • Reconciled quantities and dispatch handoff

Frequently asked questions

Can the remaining panels stay unprotected after a split?

Do not assume so. Confirm storage protection for the opened stock with the supplier.

Does a fragile sticker replace protective packing?

No. Labels communicate handling information; they do not provide physical protection.
