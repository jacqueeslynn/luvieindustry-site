SPC Flooring in Qatar: Furniture Delivery Protection

A new floor can be handed over just before furniture arrives, leaving the most demanding movement to a team that never saw the flooring instructions. For a Qatar apartment or commercial fitout using SPC, delivery protection should be agreed before installation finishes. The buyer needs a clear route, compatible protection and assigned responsibilities, not a general promise that rigid-core flooring cannot be scratched or dented.

Short answer

Coordinate furniture movement with the actual flooring care and installation guidance. Select compatible protection and suitable handling methods, then inspect the floor before and after delivery without assuming that any covering or furniture glide is acceptable.

Identify the loads and the route

Ask the furniture supplier what will be delivered, how it is packaged and which equipment is proposed. Map the route from the loading point through lifts, thresholds and rooms. In a Qatar occupied property, confirm building restrictions and the destination areas available for unpacking. Heavy or awkward items may need a specific handling plan from the responsible team. Do not prescribe a universal trolley or a number of people from a photograph. The route should also consider narrow turns where packaging, wheels or furniture edges could contact walls and floor transitions.

Obtain compatible protection guidance

Request the actual SPC manufacturer’s instructions for temporary protection and furniture movement. Tarkett care guidance is a useful example of why moving furniture and selecting protectors require attention, but its specific recommendations must not be transferred automatically to another product. Confirm whether the proposed covering, tape, mat or glide is suitable and how it should be used. Avoid trapping debris under a protective layer or assuming that a soft-looking material cannot mark the finish. The contractor should also verify any installation-related restrictions before scheduling movement over the newly completed floor.

Assign responsibility at the trade handover

Record the condition of the floor before the furniture team starts and identify who installs, checks and removes the protection. Agree where packaging will be opened and how waste will be removed without dragging it across finished surfaces. If a protective layer becomes displaced or contaminated, stop and correct the problem rather than continue because most of the route remains covered. Keep the flooring contact information available for questions, but do not expect a supplier to supervise the entire delivery. Clear responsibilities help prevent gaps between the installer, main contractor and furniture company.

Inspect and document after delivery

Remove protection using the approved method and inspect the route and final furniture positions. Check that the selected furniture feet or protectors remain suitable for their intended use and follow the actual care guidance. Record any observed damage promptly with location and photographs; do not attempt an unapproved repair to hide it. Compare the evidence with the pre-delivery record without prejudging responsibility. Retain the product identity and replacement-material information for further assessment. Good documentation supports a practical response, but it does not guarantee that a mark is repairable or that a warranty covers the event.

Before you request a quotation

  • Map furniture loads, equipment and the delivery route.
  • Confirm compatibility of protection, tape and furniture feet.
  • Assign installation, monitoring and removal responsibilities.
  • Inspect before and after delivery and record any concerns.

Frequently asked questions

Is SPC flooring immune to furniture damage?

No. Resistance claims depend on the product and do not remove the need for appropriate handling and protection.

Can any soft mat or tape be used?

No. Confirm compatibility with the actual flooring and follow its instructions.
