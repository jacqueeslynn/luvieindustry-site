WPC Wall Panels in Qatar: A Digital Sample Register

A project team can accumulate dozens of panel photographs in messages without knowing which one was approved. For Qatar distributors and fitout buyers, a digital sample register is useful when it connects the physical sample, product reference and current decision. It becomes unreliable when a cropped image or a decorative nickname is treated as the specification. The register should make uncertainty visible rather than hide it behind a tidy folder.

Short answer

Give each physical sample a stable reference and record its status, related product documents and approval context. Keep photographs as supporting information and never treat a digital colour image as proof of exact appearance or technical performance.

Create a record that survives forwarded messages

Use an ID that appears on the physical sample and its digital record. Include the supplier, product model, finish reference, date received and the project or buyer who requested it. Record whether it is an appearance sample, a profile sample or another type of demonstration. These distinctions matter because a small decorative piece may not represent the complete installed assembly. In a Qatar team working across sales, design and site roles, use the same reference in messages and quotation requests so a forwarded photograph can still be traced back to the source.

Photograph the information as well as the face

Capture the visible finish and the profile or reverse where relevant, along with the sample identification. Use consistent viewing conditions where practical and keep the original image instead of repeatedly compressing screenshots. Avoid edits that change the apparent colour or texture. Record that screen images are indicative and that physical review may still be necessary. If the supplier provides a specification or test document, link it to the exact model and preserve its stated scope. A photograph of a certificate next to a sample does not establish that the document applies to that product.

Use statuses that describe real decisions

Distinguish received, under review, appearance accepted, rejected and superseded. An appearance acceptance should not be labelled technically approved unless the appropriate review actually occurred. Record who made the decision, for which location and against which drawing or brief. If the project changes, update the status rather than silently replacing the file. This avoids a common procurement trap: a sales colleague sees an old approved image and assumes it applies to a new location. The register is an information tool, not a substitute for the responsible designer or consultant’s decision.

Keep the register useful after purchase

Link the accepted sample record to the order and available delivery identity, then record any agreed substitution. Retain the physical sample where it is needed for receiving or appearance comparisons. Store it appropriately and inspect its condition before using it as a reference again. General HSE guidance supports planning safe handling of materials but does not define sample approval criteria. At project close, archive the installed-product information and outstanding limitations. A future buyer should be able to tell whether the record describes an actual installed selection, an abandoned option or an old sample that needs reconfirmation.

Before you request a quotation

  • Match physical and digital sample IDs.
  • Record the exact model and the purpose of the sample.
  • Separate appearance acceptance from technical approval.
  • Archive superseded decisions without losing their history.

Frequently asked questions

Can a digital sample image replace physical colour review?

Not reliably. Displays, lighting and image processing affect appearance.

Does a sample marked approved cover every project location?

No. Record the location, purpose and scope of the actual acceptance.
