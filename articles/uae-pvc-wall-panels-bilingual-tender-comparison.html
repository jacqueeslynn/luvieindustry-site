PVC Wall Panels in the UAE: Bilingual Tender Checks

A tender can describe the same decorative wall in English and Arabic while leaving bidders with different interpretations of the scope. UAE buyers comparing PVC wall-panel offers should check technical meaning before ranking prices. A translation error in a profile description, accessory name or excluded installation item can be more expensive than a minor difference in the panel rate. The goal is a shared purchasing brief, not simply two polished language versions.

Short answer

Use one controlled item register with matching references in both languages. Resolve ambiguous terms against drawings and supplier data, then compare quotations on the same scope without assuming that a translated product name proves technical equivalence.

Give every tender item a stable identity

Assign an item reference that remains unchanged across the English and Arabic documents. Link it to the wall location, drawing revision and proposed product information. Avoid relying only on a translated decorative name, because a term such as panel can refer to different constructions and sizes. Include the actual dimensions, effective coverage where relevant and the intended surface use from verified information. If the tender allows alternatives, describe the approval route and required evidence. This gives each bidder a clear way to identify a substitution instead of silently pricing a different material under a similar label.

Review the terms that affect quantities

Check the meaning of sheet, piece, carton, square metre, trim and installed area in both versions. Distinguish nominal dimensions from the coverage used for the quantity calculation. If one document requests a complete system while the other appears to request panels only, correct the scope before inviting final prices. Drawings can resolve some ambiguity, but they must carry the same revision in both packages. Ask a competent bilingual reviewer to examine the technical terms rather than relying on automatic translation alone, especially where a small wording change alters the responsibility of the supplier or installer.

Separate supply from site responsibilities

List substrate preparation, supports, adhesives, trims, cutting, installation, protection and waste removal as explicit inclusions or exclusions. Identify who measures the site and who confirms product suitability. General HSE handling guidance can inform a safe-handling discussion, but it is not a UAE tender rule or a product installation specification. Do not turn a generic reference into a contractual performance claim. Where a requirement is unclear, issue the same clarification to all bidders so comparisons remain fair and the purchasing team is not comparing one complete scope with one partial offer.

Record clarifications before selecting the offer

Build a comparison table that includes the offered model, quantity basis, accessories, document availability and unresolved deviations. Keep the original supplier wording alongside any translation used for internal review. Ask for written confirmation of significant clarifications, then identify the accepted version in the purchase order. A lower headline rate is not necessarily a lower complete-project cost if necessary items are omitted. Once the order is placed, retain the bilingual item references on receiving and installation records so a site question can be traced back to the agreed scope without another round of informal translation.

Before you request a quotation

  • Use matching item references in both languages.
  • Check units, effective coverage and accessory terminology.
  • State supply and installation inclusions separately.
  • Resolve deviations in writing before comparing final totals.

Frequently asked questions

Does the same translated product name mean two offers are equivalent?

No. Compare the actual model, construction, dimensions, intended use and complete scope.

Can automatic translation be used without technical review?

It can assist drafting, but important purchasing terms and obligations need competent review.
