PU stone panels for Uzbekistan: approve the visible cut at the end wall

A decorative wall can look convincing from the front while its final cut remains conspicuous from the doorway. For an Uzbekistan showroom or hospitality interior, that exposed end deserves its own purchasing decision. A face sample alone does not show how the installed wall will terminate. Before comparing quotations, identify the visible ends and ask the supplier and installer to explain what the proposed finish includes.

Short answer

Approve a representative end-wall mockup showing the actual panel face, cut edge and proposed termination. Connect that approval to a drawing revision and written quotation scope. Do not assume that a matching face colour, an accessory photograph or a panel-only price establishes an acceptable finished edge.

Mark every end that people will see

On the layout, identify where the decorative surface stops beside a doorway, open passage or adjacent finish. Show the approach direction as well as the front elevation. Record whether the panel returns around the wall or ends on its face. Ask the site team to check the wall geometry before ordering cut pieces. For a Tashkent showroom inquiry, photographs with marked end locations give the supplier more useful context than a total wall area alone. Keep unconfirmed measurements clearly labelled so an indicative quotation is not mistaken for a cutting schedule.

Discuss options without assuming compatibility

Ask whether the offered model supports a suitable return, compatible trim or another supplier-approved end treatment. These are options to investigate, not promises that every accessory is available. GenStone's own guidance describes several end-wall approaches for its system and calls for matching paint on custom cuts. That source illustrates why an edge needs attention; its product specifications and installation requirements do not apply to Luvie panels. Obtain instructions for the exact Luvie model under consideration. Do not select paint, adhesive or filler solely because it appears in another manufacturer's guide.

Approve an edge sample under relevant lighting

Request a mockup that contains the proposed cut and finishing method, not just an untouched factory edge. View it from the normal approach and at an oblique angle under lighting representative of the intended interior. Review the transition to the adjacent surface, the exposed profile and the colour relationship between the face and end. Record what is accepted with labelled photographs and, where practical, retain the physical sample. Photographs can help document the decision but may hide texture or colour differences. A changed panel model, accessory or finishing method should trigger a new review before installation proceeds.

Make the quotation boundaries explicit

Separate panel quantities from end accessories, finishing materials, sample preparation and site labour. Ask which party supplies each item and who completes and checks the edge. Identify any site cutting, colour adjustment or rework excluded from the supply price. Do not insert a generic waste percentage before the layout and cutting approach are understood. If the buyer will source finishing materials locally in Uzbekistan, require compatibility confirmation before procurement. Keep the approved detail reference in both the quotation and purchase order so a later substitution cannot quietly replace the accepted solution.

Before you request a quotation

  • Visible ends marked on the current layout.
  • Exact model and proposed edge treatment identified.
  • Representative mockup accepted and documented.
  • Materials, labour and exclusions separated in the offer.

Frequently asked questions

Is a factory-finished sample enough?

Not when the installed design exposes a custom cut. Request a sample that demonstrates the proposed finished cut.

Does appearance approval confirm technical suitability?

No. Obtain separate confirmation of the offered model's suitability and installation requirements for the intended location.
