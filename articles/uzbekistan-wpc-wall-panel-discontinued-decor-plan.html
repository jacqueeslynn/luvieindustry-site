Plan for a discontinued WPC décor in Uzbekistan

A discontinued WPC décor in Uzbekistan can leave a distributor with active samples, open quotes and remaining stock that no longer share the same commercial status. The issue is not only which replacement colour looks closest. Buyers need to know what can still be supplied, which commitments remain open and where the old finish has already been installed.

Short answer

Separate confirmed stock from future availability, stop unsupported repeat-order promises and review alternatives against the actual project requirements. Preserve the old model’s identity and records; a replacement décor should not quietly inherit them.

Confirm what discontinued actually means

Ask whether production has ended, a surface option has changed or only a particular packing format is unavailable. Request a dated statement of what the supplier can currently offer without assuming a final purchase opportunity exists. The distributor should distinguish physical stock already held from unconfirmed future supply. A vague message that a colour is changing is not enough to update every customer commitment.

Find the affected commercial records

Search open quotations, reserved projects, showroom samples and dealer price lists for the model. Identify which offers are still provisional and which have become accepted commitments requiring direct commercial review. Do not continue advertising normal availability simply because a sample remains on the display wall. For multi-city distribution, tell each sales team the same status so customers do not receive conflicting answers from different branches.

Compare replacements as new selections

Review the alternative’s profile, surface, dimensions, accessory compatibility and installation information, not just its colour. A close appearance may still create a visible mismatch beside existing material. Ask the buyer whether the replacement is for a separate room or an extension of completed work. The latter can require a more careful visual review. Do not promise an invisible match based on a screen image or a small unlabelled offcut.

Preserve the original identity

GS1’s product-management standard provides a useful reference for distinguishing new or changed products. Keep the discontinued model’s records and photographs available for identification, while marking its sales status clearly. Assign the replacement its own reference in your internal system. This prevents future staff from using a new product’s documentation to answer questions about an older installation merely because the marketing names were merged.

Agree the stock and service plan

Count remaining stock accurately and decide how it will be offered under the applicable commercial terms. Keep partial or opened cartons identifiable. Ask existing project buyers whether they have an approved spare requirement, but do not imply stock is guaranteed until confirmed. Retire obsolete samples from active selection and retain a labelled reference for history. A controlled exit from the range is more useful than an urgent sales message unsupported by actual quantities.

Before you request a quotation

  • Clarify production, stock and quotation status separately.
  • Identify affected quotes and installed projects.
  • Review alternatives as distinct products.
  • Retain historical records and retire active samples.

Frequently asked questions

Can a similar colour replace the old décor automatically?

No. Review profile, surface, dimensions and the project’s visual requirements. Similar appearance does not establish full equivalence.

Should the old model disappear from the system?

Keep it identifiable for historical orders and installed work, while clearly marking its current availability status.
