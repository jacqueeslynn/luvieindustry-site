PVC wall panels for Peru: compare what the quotation actually includes

Two PVC wall panel quotations can look comparable because both show a price per square metre. One may cover panels alone, while the other includes profiles or a local installation service. For a distributor buying for Lima or another Peruvian destination, the useful comparison starts with a common supply schedule, not the lowest headline number. This guide explains how to turn mismatched offers into a decision record without inventing local prices or import charges.

Short answer

Give every bidder the same panel specification, layout and delivery boundary. Separate confirmed inclusions, priced options and exclusions. Compare totals only after the quantities and scope describe the same work.

Identify the panel before comparing the rate

Record the product code, material construction, finish, nominal dimensions and useful installed coverage. A marble-effect sheet, a hollow interlocking profile and a fluted decorative panel are not interchangeable simply because a seller calls each one PVC. Ask whether the price unit is a piece, carton, nominal square metre or covered square metre. Keep the conversion and carton rounding visible. A sample approval should identify the exact item rather than a general colour family.

Build one accessory schedule for every offer

Mark wall ends, internal and external corners, openings and transitions on a simple drawing. List required trims by profile and length, alongside panels and any specified installation materials. The Peruvian TECNOPVC brochure presents a wall and ceiling product system with profiles; it is a useful reminder to look beyond the main panel, not evidence that its components fit Luvie products. Ask each supplier to mark included, optional or not supplied against every line. An empty cell is an unanswered question, not a zero cost.

Separate supply from local site work

An overseas material offer and a Peruvian installed quotation have different boundaries. Ask the local contractor to identify substrate preparation, removal of existing finishes, access equipment, installation, protection and waste removal separately where applicable. Do not assume the panel seller has inspected the wall or approved the fixing method. For delivery, identify the named destination, unloading responsibility and any onward transport. Have the relevant logistics and tax professionals confirm charges for the actual transaction rather than copying a percentage from another order.

Resolve alternatives before the purchase order

Maintain an exceptions column for changed lengths, finishes, accessories and proposed substitutes. If an offer replaces the selected profile with another construction, treat that as a technical alternative needing approval, not an automatic saving. Compare the accepted scope, currency basis, validity and separately confirmed commercial terms. Keep unresolved items open until the responsible party answers. Attach the final schedule, drawing revision and sample reference to the order so receiving staff can check the same specification that purchasing approved.

Before you request a quotation

  • Exact panel code and price-unit conversion
  • Common trim and accessory schedule
  • Written site-work and delivery exclusions
  • Approved alternatives and final revision

Frequently asked questions

Can I compare quotations by panel price alone?

Only if the product, coverage, quantities and supply boundaries are already equivalent. Otherwise the apparent difference may be omitted scope.

Does a blank accessory line mean it is included?

No. Obtain an explicit inclusion, separate price or exclusion before approving the comparison.
