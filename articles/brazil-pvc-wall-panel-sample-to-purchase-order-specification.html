Brazil PVC Wall Panels: Turn a Sample into an Order

A Brazilian distributor can approve an attractive PVC wall-panel sample and still receive an order that is difficult to evaluate. The missing link is often a written definition of what the sample represents. This guide turns a showroom decision into a practical specification that purchasing, receiving and the installer can all use.

Short answer

Treat the approved sample as one part of an order dossier, not the whole contract. Connect it to a product code, drawing, finish reference, accessory list and agreed inspection method.

Independent reference

ISO: quality management systems. General guidance, not a Luvie product certification.

Separate appearance from construction

Start with two approvals. The appearance approval records the chosen surface, sheen and visible pattern. The construction approval identifies profile shape, nominal dimensions, connected coverage and installation method. A small decorative chip cannot show the joint between two full panels or how a termination trim fits. Request a short connected assembly and photograph both faces. Record who approved it and retain a physical reference in Brazil. This prevents a later discussion about colour from concealing a more basic change in profile or usable width.

Make the Portuguese order schedule unambiguous

Use one controlled schedule with a supplier code, your local sales code and a plain Portuguese description on each line. Keep the original technical term beside a translation when its meaning could change. List pieces and cartons separately from square metres; state whether area is nominal or effective connected coverage. Put accessories on their own lines rather than hiding them in a description such as complete system. A revised drawing needs a revision number and date, so an earlier WhatsApp attachment cannot silently become the production reference.

Define what receiving staff can actually verify

Agree practical acceptance evidence before payment milestones: carton identification, quantity reconciliation, visible condition, profile comparison and finish comparison under stated viewing conditions. Ask the supplier to explain any declared tolerances and how they are measured; do not invent a universal tolerance. Keep destructive laboratory testing separate from warehouse visual checks. ISO describes ISO 9001 as a quality-management-system standard. A management certificate may inform supplier assessment, but it does not replace the ordered product specification or prove a particular fire, emissions or durability result.

Control changes after sample approval

Require proposed substitutions to be identified before shipment, including changes to the finish reference, backing, profile, packing or accessories. Decide who in your company can accept a change and how that acceptance is recorded. When the sales team requests an additional colour, create a new approval line instead of treating it as identical to the first one. Keep the final schedule with the commercial invoice and packing list for internal comparison. This is an operational record, not a substitute for the importer and broker checking Brazilian requirements.

Before you request a quotation

  • Retain an identified physical sample and connected joint.
  • Match supplier and local item codes in one schedule.
  • Record coverage units, accessories and drawing revision.
  • Agree inspection evidence and substitution approval ownership.

Frequently asked questions

Does approving a sample guarantee every later batch is identical?

No. Record the agreed appearance reference and variation criteria, then verify each received batch against the order.

Is a supplier ISO certificate enough?

No. It concerns a management system; obtain evidence relevant to the actual product and application.
