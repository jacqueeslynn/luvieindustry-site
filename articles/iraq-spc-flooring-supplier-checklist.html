Choosing an SPC flooring supplier for Iraq: a buyer checklist

Searching for an SPC flooring supplier for Iraq produces plenty of claims about quality and low prices. The more useful question is which supplier can turn your project brief into an identifiable product, an inspectable order and a workable delivery plan. This checklist is for importers comparing offers, not a ranking of unverified manufacturers.

Short answer

Shortlist suppliers by sample-to-order consistency, relevant documentation and clear responsibilities. A convincing website or a low price does not prove that the quoted flooring fits your project or import route.

Independent reference

International Trade Administration: Iraq import documentation. General guidance, not a Luvie product certification.

Begin with the destination and the actual application

State the Iraqi delivery city, intended entry route and whether the flooring is for resale, apartments or a commercial project. Give the supplier the project requirements rather than asking for a generic “Iraq grade.” Do not prescribe one specification for the whole country. Building conditions, substrate and use matter. Ask who will review technical suitability locally and which documents that reviewer needs before a sample is accepted. Keep the sales finish selection separate from project approval.

Request an identifiable sample package

Ask for a model code, construction details, installation instructions and available reports tied to that construction. Check several connected planks rather than judging only a small color chip. Confirm whether backing is included and whether extra underlay is permitted. Record appearance approval with the reference and date. If reports name another product, ask for the connection in writing; a logo on a brochure is not enough. Retain the accepted sample so pre-shipment inspection has a physical reference.

Resolve Iraq documentation with the importer before payment

The International Trade Administration publishes an Iraq documentation overview, but it is written for US exporters and is not a complete rulebook for shipments from other origins. Ask your Iraqi importer or broker to verify current requirements for the goods, origin and entry point. Request a draft commercial invoice and packing list for review, and establish responsibility for any required origin or conformity documents. Do not assume that a sample shipment and a commercial order follow identical procedures.

Use a clear acceptance process instead of a supplier ranking

Give every candidate the same unanswered questions and compare the quality of their responses. Before the order, record inspection criteria, packing, delivery scope, payment milestones and the procedure for reporting differences. Confirm the named contracting entity and verify payment details through an independently confirmed contact if they change. Release the order only after commercial and technical exceptions are resolved. The aim is not to find a supplier calling itself “best,” but one whose offer can be checked before and after shipment.

Before you request a quotation

  • Iraqi destination and entry route
  • Product-coded samples and relevant reports
  • Importer-approved draft documents
  • Written inspection and discrepancy process

Frequently asked questions

Does an overseas test report guarantee Iraqi acceptance?

No. Check its scope and the destination or project requirements with the responsible local parties.

Should I request samples before a bulk order?

Yes, where practicable, and keep the accepted reference. Sample approval does not replace production inspection.
