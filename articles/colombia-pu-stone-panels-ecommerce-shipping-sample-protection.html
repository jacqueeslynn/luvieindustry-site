PU Stone Parcels in Colombia: Protect Decorative Relief

A Colombian distributor selling PU stone-look panels in small online orders faces a different handling pattern from a project pallet delivery. Individual parcels may pass through several sorting and delivery stages. The buyer should review how the decorative relief, exposed edges and accessories are protected without assuming that low weight means damage cannot occur.

Short answer

Validate the parcel format with the product supplier and delivery provider. Protect the actual panel geometry, identify every piece and use a receiving process that records both the packaging and the material.

Independent reference

IMO/ILO/UNECE CTU Code. General guidance, not a Luvie product certification.

Start with the real parcel geometry

Record the proposed number of panels, packed dimensions and weight using confirmed information. Identify projecting relief, vulnerable corners and any accessories placed in the same package. Ask the carrier about acceptance conditions for the actual parcel format rather than relying on a general small-parcel price. The IMO CTU Code is broader intermodal packing guidance, not certification of this e-commerce parcel. Its general handling perspective is useful, but the supplier and carrier still need to review the specific packaging and delivery arrangement.

Protect the face without creating new pressure points

Ask the supplier how the relief surface and edges should be supported and separated. A packing material that protects a flat face may contact only the highest points of a textured panel. Do not prescribe a universal foam thickness or stacking load without testing and guidance. Keep sharp accessories or tools away from decorative faces, and identify them in a separate protected compartment if included. Review the package after opening as well as before dispatch, because a difficult unpacking method can create damage that the outer carton never experienced.

Run a documented trial before scaling the offer

Use a representative shipment or an appropriate packaging evaluation agreed with the relevant parties. Record the panel identity, package construction, carrier service and observed condition at arrival. A successful trial is useful feedback, not proof that every future parcel will arrive intact. If damage occurs, distinguish outer crushing, edge contact and surface abrasion so the packaging can be revised intelligently. Avoid presenting an unverified packaging concept as a tested guarantee on the online product page.

Make the customer’s receiving steps clear

Provide a short Spanish instruction asking the receiver to photograph the unopened package, labels and any damage before discarding material. Keep the procedure practical and consistent with the carrier and seller’s agreed process; do not invent legal claim deadlines. Link the order to its product and batch record so replacements can be assessed accurately. Publish realistic delivery and damage-handling information only after it is confirmed. This supports the Colombian distributor’s service process without promising damage-proof panels or an automatic colour match from future stock.

Before you request a quotation

  • Confirm packed dimensions and carrier acceptance.
  • Protect relief faces and edges without pressure points.
  • Record a representative packaging trial and limitations.
  • Give clear receiving instructions with traceable order identity.

Frequently asked questions

Does a lightweight panel need less packaging automatically?

No. Surface geometry, edges and the handling chain still matter; packaging must suit the actual product.

Does one successful parcel prove the format is damage-proof?

No. It is evidence from that trial and should inform, not replace, ongoing review.
