Check UV wall reflections near office screens

UV marble wall reflections in Uzbekistan offices are best reviewed before a glossy finish is ordered for a large area. A surface that looks elegant in a sample photograph may reflect windows or lighting into a workstation. This is not an argument against a particular material: it is a reason to assess the finish in the actual room arrangement.

Short answer

Review a representative sample from seated working positions with the intended screens, windows and lighting. Compare possible layout or finish changes with the designer. A small visual trial helps make a selection but is not a laboratory optical-performance test.

Independent reference

WorkSafe Queensland: visual comfort for computer work. General guidance, not a Luvie product certification.

Map the people and screens first

Identify where people sit, which direction screens face and which walls fall within their normal view. Include movable desks if the office layout is expected to change. A reception backdrop viewed briefly has a different relationship to occupants than a wall beside a monitor used all day. Ask the project team which positions should govern the review rather than judging the finish only from the doorway.

Bring a meaningful sample to the room

Use the selected finish at a representative orientation and keep any protective film status clear. A tiny swatch may show colour but not the scale of a reflection. Ask whether a larger sample or temporary mock-up can be reviewed safely. Do not interpret a sample covered by a protective layer as the final exposed surface. Record the exact model so a later substituted finish does not inherit the same approval.

Review more than one lighting condition

Check the relevant daylight and artificial-light situations available during the review. WorkSafe Queensland’s visual-comfort guidance notes that reflections can come from screens, work surfaces and surrounding finishes. It is general workstation guidance, not a rating for UV sheets or an Uzbekistan compliance certificate. Ask the designer whether blinds, luminaire position, screen orientation or a different finish can address an observed problem without compromising other project needs.

Document the observation without inventing metrics

Photographs can support the review, but camera exposure may exaggerate or hide a reflection. Record the viewing position and the practical concern in ordinary language. Do not assign a gloss value or a glare rating without the relevant measurement. If a formal performance requirement exists, obtain the appropriate technical information or specialist assessment. A buyer’s visual preference and a measured requirement should remain distinguishable in the approval record.

Preserve the accepted arrangement

Include the chosen finish and any related lighting or furniture decisions in the handover. If the office later moves a row of screens or removes blinds, the original review may no longer describe the conditions. For a distributor, this is a useful question during quotation: where will the reflective wall sit relative to regular occupants? It encourages a more informed choice than promising that every decorative finish works equally well in every room.

Before you request a quotation

  • Review from seated screen-working positions.
  • Use the selected exposed finish and orientation.
  • Consider relevant daylight and artificial lighting.
  • Record layout decisions with the finish approval.

Frequently asked questions

Are glossy UV sheets unsuitable for every office?

No. Suitability depends on the layout, lighting and use. Review the actual arrangement rather than making a universal judgement.

Can a photograph prove visual comfort?

No. It can document the review, but occupants’ viewing positions and appropriate specialist assessment remain important where required.
