WPC panels for Qatar: record the gaps in a remote video inspection

A live video call can let a Qatari buyer ask questions while someone shows WPC panels at the dispatch location. It can also create false confidence if the viewer sees attractive pieces but cannot connect them to the order or tell which checks were omitted. The procurement task is to define what the session can establish and preserve the unanswered questions. This guide proposes evidence controls for a buyer-organized call, not an accredited inspection service or a promise that remote viewing can replace every physical check.

Short answer

Agree the order references and inspection questions before the call. Connect the overview, carton identity and selected pieces through a clear viewing sequence, then record each completed or uncompleted checkpoint. If the connection, camera position or available equipment prevents a reliable observation, mark the result as not verified and arrange a suitable follow-up. A smooth presentation is not a blanket shipment-release decision.

Set the scope and the people before opening the camera

List the purchase-order revision, WPC references and the questions to be addressed. Identify the remote reviewer, on-site presenter and person authorized to decide the next step. Ask whether the session is an informal supplier demonstration, a contracted inspection or another defined service; do not describe them interchangeably. SGS's remote-inspection service overview shows that remote interaction can be part of a structured service. It does not imply that an ordinary video call receives SGS endorsement or certification. Decide which records the participants may share or retain, obtain appropriate consent for recording and keep unrelated customer information outside the view. Confirm a fallback if connectivity is insufficient.

Preserve the link between location, label and piece

Start with enough overview to understand the inspection area, then move to the relevant order identification and the selected carton. Agree who selects the cartons and pieces rather than allowing a collection of preselected display samples to stand for the order without disclosure. Where practical, maintain a continuous view while the presenter moves from the identifier to the piece being reviewed. If the camera cuts away, reconnect the identity visibly before continuing. Ask for readable labels and contextual still images when needed. A continuous shot helps the viewer follow the sequence, but it does not prove the origin or condition of every carton outside the frame.

Separate what was observed from what was assumed

Use a checkpoint record with the question, visible evidence and outcome. Distinguish a directly observed feature from a statement made by the presenter. Colour, texture and fine surface details may need a physical sample or another agreed assessment rather than acceptance from a compressed image. For measurements, ask the responsible inspector to show the sample identity, method and reading; a close-up of digits alone does not establish where the instrument touched. Do not ask an untrained presenter to improvise destructive or hazardous checks for the camera. Record inaccessible surfaces, missing equipment, poor lighting and dropped connections as evidence gaps instead of filling them with optimistic conclusions.

Close with exceptions and a separate release decision

Read back the unresolved checkpoints and assign an owner to each follow-up. Agree whether a repeat session, clearer document, retained sample or on-site inspection is needed, without assuming that every gap can be closed remotely. Keep relevant images and notes linked to the order and state the actual scope and date of the session. The authorized buyer should then make a separate decision under the agreed purchasing process: proceed, hold or request further evidence. Do not convert a presenter saying everything looks good into approval of undisplayed quantities or characteristics. If the goods or order change after the call, review whether the previous observations still apply before relying on them.

Before you request a quotation

  • Order revision, roles and permitted recording agreed.
  • Carton and piece identity connected through the viewing sequence.
  • Observed, stated and unverified checkpoints distinguished.
  • Exceptions assigned and purchase release decided separately.

Frequently asked questions

Does an uninterrupted video prove the whole shipment is acceptable?

No. It may improve continuity for the items shown, but it does not establish the condition of unseen material or replace checks outside the agreed scope.

Should a blurred checkpoint be marked as passed if the presenter confirms it?

No. Record the statement separately and leave the observation unverified until suitable evidence is available under the agreed process.
