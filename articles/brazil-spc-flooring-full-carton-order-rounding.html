SPC flooring for Brazil: turn measured floor area into a clear carton order

A floor measurement and a purchase quantity answer different questions. The first describes the space to cover; the second describes the material being delivered. For a Brazil SPC flooring project, a quotation expressed per square metre can obscure this distinction if the supplier sells only complete cartons. Establish the calculation before comparing offers, and keep the resulting physical surplus visible in the purchasing record.

Short answer

Record net area, add the installer-approved cutting allowance and any agreed maintenance reserve, then divide by the confirmed coverage per carton. Round the result upward to the next whole carton when full-carton sale applies. Confirm whether compatible rooms can share one material pool and document who receives the remaining boards.

Start with measured area, not a shopping-cart total

List each room and its measured net area, noting exclusions and the drawing revision. Ask the installer to resolve uncertain boundaries, recesses and areas beneath fixed fittings. Keep this measurement separate from allowances. Cutting needs depend on the layout, plank direction and installation plan; this guide does not prescribe a universal waste percentage. A maintenance reserve is also a separate purchasing decision, not automatically installation waste. Recording these quantities individually makes later changes explainable.

Confirm the carton behind the square-metre price

Ask for the offered model, finish, plank dimensions, pieces per carton and declared coverage per carton in the current quotation. Tarkett Brazil's Ambienta Series document illustrates how a manufacturer identifies packaging alongside technical specifications. It concerns that manufacturer's vinyl products, not Luvie SPC: its packaging, dimensions and performance values must not be transferred to a Luvie order. Obtain Luvie's own confirmed data for the exact reference. Also clarify whether the commercial sale unit is a carton and whether the quoted square-metre price is only a comparison basis.

Combine rooms only when the material can actually be shared

Where rooms use the same approved product and compatible supply batch, ask the installer whether their material requirements can be pooled before carton rounding. Rounding every room separately can produce more cartons than rounding one combined requirement. However, pooling must not assume that every offcut is reusable: length, joint position and installation sequence may prevent reuse. Keep different finishes, constructions or separately controlled deliveries on distinct calculation lines. Show both the room allocation and the consolidated purchasing quantity so site staff know which cartons belong to the shared pool.

Explain the rounding surplus and its destination

Use a simple calculation: required area equals net area plus agreed allowances; carton count equals required area divided by confirmed carton coverage, rounded upward. Purchased coverage equals carton count multiplied by carton coverage. The difference between purchased coverage and required area is the rounding surplus, not proof of defective measurement. Before ordering, agree whether unopened cartons and usable loose boards remain with the client, building operator or another named party. Record the handover location, labeling and inventory responsibility. Do not assume unused material is returnable or that an installer may retain it; confirm those commercial arrangements explicitly.

Before you request a quotation

  • Measured net area and drawing revision recorded.
  • Allowance and maintenance reserve approved separately.
  • Exact carton coverage and sale unit confirmed.
  • Room pooling, surplus ownership and handover agreed.

Frequently asked questions

Can I order the exact measured square metres?

Only if the supplier permits that sale quantity. For full-carton orders, the purchased coverage normally follows the carton calculation rather than the net measurement.

Is rounding surplus the same as cutting waste?

No. Rounding surplus arises from the packaging increment; cutting waste arises during installation. Keep both concepts separate when reconciling the delivery and final remaining stock.
