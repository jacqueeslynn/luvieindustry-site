SPC flooring for Chile: inspect returned click joints before reallocating stock

An installer may return a few spare cartons after a refurbishment, while a showroom returns loose planks used for a demonstration. Both can look clean from above, but they do not have the same handling history. For Chilean distributors, the procurement question is not simply whether the colour still matches. It is whether the exact returned material can be identified, assessed and allocated without concealing uncertainty from the next buyer. This guide addresses locking-edge evidence and stock decisions, not consumer return rights or a promise that used material can be resold.

Short answer

Keep returned planks outside available stock until their identity, installation history and locking-edge condition have been reviewed against the selected manufacturer's instructions. Record who decides whether they remain on hold, become demonstration material or can be released with appropriate disclosure. A clean decorative face does not establish that a click joint is suitable for another installation.

Separate unopened cartons from assembled demonstration planks

At receipt, create separate records for unopened surplus cartons, opened but reportedly uninstalled planks, and planks that have been clicked together or removed from a floor. Ask the returning party which category applies and whether the material was cut, transported loose or mixed with another order. Record uncertainty rather than converting an incomplete answer into an unused-stock label. Photograph packaging labels and the plank ends before repacking. Keep quantities in cartons and individual pieces distinct so a later stock count does not treat a partly filled carton as complete. Assign a return identifier that stays with the material during assessment and any subsequent movement.

Make the locking edge the inspection subject

Lay out a controlled assessment area where the person reviewing the return can see the long and short edges without damaging them further. Record visible chips, crushed corners, contamination and differences between pieces, using photographs that include the return identifier. Do not force a questionable plank into an approved sample to see whether it will fit: that can damage the reference and does not establish long-term performance. Ask the supplier which inspection method and acceptance information apply to the exact model. If instructions do not address disassembly or reuse, retain that as an unresolved question. An improvised visual inspection is useful evidence, not a substitute for manufacturer acceptance criteria.

Keep identity and disposition linked

The stock record should connect the returned pieces to the original product reference, available batch markings and the customer's account of their handling. Do not infer compatibility from similar colour, dimensions or packaging alone. Keep the inspection result and supplier correspondence beside the stock identifier so the sales desk sees the hold before quoting the material. A quality-management reference can help frame documented decisions, but ISO information does not approve a particular returned SPC plank or establish Luvie certification. These operational recommendations are proposed purchasing controls, not quoted ISO requirements. Where identity cannot be established, do not quietly merge the pieces into a current product line.

Approve the next destination before changing availability

Choose a documented destination for the return rather than allowing it to drift back into pickable stock. Options may include continued quarantine, a clearly identified display allocation, or another disposition agreed with the relevant supplier and responsible buyer. A demonstration allocation should remain visibly separate from saleable material and should not be represented as a factory-fresh performance sample. If release for a project is being considered, record the evidence, limitations, disclosure and person authorizing that decision before changing the inventory status. Tell warehouse staff which identifier and documents must accompany the pieces. Review repeated returns for a pattern of handling damage, but do not claim that a small internal sample proves a manufacturing defect.

Before you request a quotation

  • Original product identity and return identifier linked.
  • Unopened, uninstalled and previously assembled pieces separated.
  • Locking-edge observations and supplier questions recorded.
  • Disposition owner and inventory-release authority named.

Frequently asked questions

Does an undamaged decorative surface prove the plank can be reused?

No. The visible face does not establish the condition or suitability of the locking edges. Review the actual model and handling history before a stock decision.

Can returned planks automatically become showroom samples?

No. Assign that use deliberately, identify the pieces as returned demonstration material and avoid presenting them as new reference samples or proof of performance.
