SPC flooring for UK retail: compare slip-test evidence before accepting the specification

A UK retail buyer may receive two SPC flooring quotations carrying different slip-related descriptions. One offers a short rating, while another attaches a laboratory report. Neither format alone tells the purchasing team whether the proposed finish suits the intended shop. Before comparing prices, ask the project’s competent flooring or safety adviser to define the evidence needed for the actual location and use. This guide addresses the document handoff into procurement, not how to perform a slip test. It does not assign a slip rating to Luvie flooring or approve any model for a particular retail area.

Short answer

Request evidence tied to the exact offered model and surface finish. Record the test method, specimen identity, condition and complete result, then let the competent project reviewer determine relevance to the proposed use. Keep unresolved questions visible in the quotation comparison. Do not replace a missing assessment with a general anti-slip label or a supplier’s verbal reassurance.

Define the area before requesting a rating

Mark the proposed floor zones on a simple project plan: sales area, entrance route, staff space or another identified location. Describe expected use and any conditions the project team needs to investigate, without assuming that one assessment covers the entire premises. HSE’s guidance treats slip potential as a combination of factors and calls for competent testing and interpretation. Its discussion includes dry and contaminated conditions. Use that context to ask better questions, not to choose a universal acceptance number yourself. Ask the responsible adviser to confirm the current methods and requirements relevant to the project’s UK location before purchasing sets the evidence request.

Connect each report to the offered surface

Create one record for each proposed model and finish, showing the quotation reference, sample identifier and supporting report. Ask the supplier to explain any differences between the report’s specimen description and the current offer. Similar colour, overall thickness or a shared SPC product-family name is not a documented match of the walking surface. Keep the original report with its pages, dates and limitations rather than copying only a result into a sales spreadsheet. If the product has changed since testing, ask what changed and route the answer to the reviewer. An unanswered identity question should remain open even when the price and appearance are attractive.

Compare the test basis, not just the headline

Put the method and test conditions beside each result so the reviewer can see whether the offers are genuinely comparable. Record whether a statement describes a laboratory sample or an installed floor, and retain any information about surface preparation and test direction provided in the report. Do not convert between unlike classifications using an informal online table. Ask the reviewer what additional evidence, if any, would resolve the gap, and who should commission it. Where a supplier offers only a brochure claim, mark the evidence as missing rather than assigning a guessed result. Confirm the review decision in writing before calling an option technically equivalent.

Carry the decision into the order and handover

Attach the accepted product reference and evidence-review decision to the purchasing record, including any limitations on where the flooring may be considered. Distinguish approval to obtain a sample from approval to order the full quantity. If additional assessment is required after installation, identify its owner and place in the project programme; a purchase order does not perform that assessment. Give the operator the relevant product care information and the project team’s agreed handover documents. Ask for renewed review when the offered surface or intended location changes. Keep unresolved safety questions with the competent team rather than asking sales staff to promise that slips cannot happen.

Before you request a quotation

  • Floor zones and intended use described for the project reviewer.
  • Exact offered surface connected to complete evidence records.
  • Methods, conditions and identity gaps recorded without guessed equivalence.
  • Purchasing decision, limitations and later review responsibilities documented.

Frequently asked questions

Does a dry test establish suitability where the floor may become wet?

Do not assume it does. Give the competent reviewer the actual use conditions and complete evidence so they can determine whether further assessment is needed before selection.

Can a supplier’s anti-slip description replace the report?

No. Request the basis of the statement and its link to the exact offered model. A marketing term does not resolve missing method, condition or specimen information.
