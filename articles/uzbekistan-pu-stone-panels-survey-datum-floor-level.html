Uzbekistan PU stone panels: reconcile the survey datum before releasing quantities

A wall height measured from the unfinished slab can differ from the height shown above the future finished floor. If those numbers enter the same PU stone-panel quantity sheet without explanation, the buyer may order to the wrong starting line or approve a layout that no longer meets the skirting and door details. For an Uzbekistan interior project, the practical task is to agree the measurement reference and resolve conflicting dimensions before the order is released, not to select a universal installation gap.

Short answer

Use one documented project datum and distinguish existing measured surfaces from proposed finished surfaces. Link each wall dimension to its location, drawing revision and measurement basis. Have the responsible design and site teams resolve discrepancies before converting the approved elevation into panel quantities and cutting assumptions.

State where every measurement starts and ends

Identify the wall by room and elevation, then record whether its height was measured from slab, existing floor or an established finished-floor reference. State the upper termination as well: visible ceiling, proposed ceiling line or another approved boundary. Keep units explicit and record the date and person who measured. A photograph of a tape can support a reading, but it rarely explains the whole reference system. Do not combine dimensions taken from different bases as if they described the same finished area. Where the finished level is not yet confirmed, mark the assumption and its owner rather than allowing a provisional number to look final.

Coordinate the adjacent finishes before freezing the elevation

Ask the project team to confirm the floor build-up, skirting approach, ceiling termination and relevant door or joinery interfaces. The WBDG systems-integration guidance emphasizes coordinating building elements and user requirements; it does not prescribe dimensions for a particular PU panel installation or establish Uzbekistan requirements. Treat it as a general coordination principle. The responsible professionals must determine the actual detail and any applicable constraints. Record which adjoining work is complete and which remains proposed. If an interface changes, compare the affected elevation and quantity assumptions rather than assuming the decorative panel can absorb every difference without a design decision.

Reconcile drawing and site differences visibly

Create a discrepancy list showing the drawing dimension, site reading, measurement basis and proposed resolution for each affected wall. Check whether an apparent conflict is simply a different reference level before requesting additional material. Do not average incompatible dimensions or silently replace a drawing value in a spreadsheet. Ask the authorized project reviewer to confirm the governing layout and document any revised termination or cut arrangement. Where a wall is irregular, request measurements sufficient for the actual layout rather than representing it with one convenient height. Keep surveying and technical design decisions with the competent project team; this purchasing guide does not authorize structural or building-service alterations.

Release a quantity sheet that preserves its assumptions

Convert only the agreed elevation into panel and accessory quantities using confirmed product dimensions and the selected layout. Identify full pieces, planned cuts and any agreed allowance separately so later readers can understand the calculation. Link the quantity sheet to the approved measurement record and drawing revision. Before issuing the purchase order, check whether a floor or ceiling change has occurred since the review. If final measurements are unavailable, obtain an explicit project decision about the remaining uncertainty rather than describing the quantity as verified. At installation handover, provide the approved starting line and references so the site team does not recreate the layout from an outdated quotation sketch.

Before you request a quotation

  • Measurement datum, units and wall locations recorded.
  • Finished-floor and adjoining-finish assumptions assigned owners.
  • Drawing/site discrepancies resolved through a documented decision.
  • Quantity release linked to the approved elevation revision.

Frequently asked questions

Can the unfinished slab height be used as the finished wall height?

Not without checking the intended floor and upper termination. Label the measurement basis and have the project team confirm the finished layout before calculating the final requirement.

Does this guide specify a standard bottom gap for PU panels?

No. The correct detail depends on the selected product and project interfaces. Obtain product-specific guidance and the responsible designer’s approved arrangement rather than copying a generic dimension.
