PVC Panels in Chile: Plan Warehouse Storage First

A Chilean distributor buying long PVC wall panels should confirm the warehouse arrangement before choosing a large assortment. The right storage method depends on the product and packaging, not simply on free floor area. This guide helps the purchasing team ask questions that protect identification and handling without inventing a universal stacking limit.

Short answer

Obtain the supplier’s storage instructions and reconcile them with the actual rack, handling equipment and picking route. Keep product support, worker safety and stock traceability as separate checks.

Independent reference

IMO/ILO/UNECE CTU Code. General guidance, not a Luvie product certification.

Survey the route from vehicle to storage

Measure the vehicle approach, unloading space, door opening and turning area for the actual packaged length. Confirm which equipment and trained operators will handle the packs. A carton that fits on a drawing may still be difficult to turn into a narrow aisle. Ask the logistics provider how the load will arrive, including pallet or bundle arrangement and any unloading constraints. The IMO CTU Code provides general intermodal handling and packing guidance; it does not approve a particular rack, lifting method or panel stack.

Ask for product-specific support instructions

Request written guidance on storage orientation, support positions, permitted stacking and environmental protection for the proposed packaging. Share rack drawings with the supplier and have the warehouse operator assess rack capacity and safe use. Do not choose a vertical or horizontal method merely because another distributor uses it for a different profile. Record the package weight and dimensions from confirmed information. If cartons are opened for partial orders, establish how the remaining pieces will be supported and protected without relying on the original full-pack arrangement.

Keep the identity visible after opening

Assign a location to each product and batch so similar finishes do not become mixed. Preserve carton labels or transfer their information to the remaining pack. Record pieces removed rather than counting only unopened cartons. A distributor serving several Chilean branches should avoid sending unlabelled loose pieces that cannot be reconciled with the receiving branch’s quotation. Set aside damaged or uncertain stock from saleable material, and make the reason visible in the internal record. This is particularly useful when an offcut resembles a shorter saleable piece.

Plan picking around the actual sales unit

Decide whether customers buy full cartons, individual pieces or project kits, then test a typical pick before the first large delivery. Include trims and accessories in the route without placing small components where they can damage faces. Keep protective packaging until the appropriate handling stage according to supplier guidance. Review stock condition during routine inventory checks and record any change in storage conditions. The goal is a repeatable process that preserves the supplied material, not an unsupported promise that panels will tolerate any warehouse temperature or stacking pressure.

Before you request a quotation

  • Confirm packaged length and unloading access.
  • Obtain written support and stacking guidance.
  • Keep batch identity on partial packs.
  • Test the actual carton or piece-picking workflow.

Frequently asked questions

Is vertical storage always wrong?

No universal answer applies. Use the specific manufacturer’s instructions and a safe warehouse design.

Can opened cartons keep their original quantity in inventory?

No. Record the actual remaining pieces and preserve their product and batch identity.
