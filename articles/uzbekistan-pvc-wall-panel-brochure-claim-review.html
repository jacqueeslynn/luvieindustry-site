Review PVC brochure claims before dealer circulation

PVC wall panel brochure claims in Uzbekistan can grow stronger each time a catalogue is copied or translated. A statement about one tested model becomes a headline for an entire range, or a decorative example becomes an alleged completed project. Importers can reduce this risk by reviewing the evidence behind each important statement before circulating sales material.

Short answer

Connect every technical or provenance claim to the exact product and supporting evidence. Remove or qualify statements that the available documents do not establish. This is an editorial control process, not a substitute for local legal or professional review.

Create a claim inventory

Read the cover, specification table, captions and sales messages together. List statements about performance, composition, certification, factory identity, project history and commercial terms. Include claims inside images rather than checking only body text. A visually small badge can communicate a stronger promise than a careful paragraph. Mark which statements are factual, which are appearance descriptions and which are proposals that still need confirmation.

Match evidence to the selected model

For each technical statement, identify the relevant document, model, date and scope. Do not use a report for another construction as a general range certificate. Where a document is unclear, ask the supplier to explain its relationship to the offered product. A request for clarification is more useful than replacing one uncertain adjective with another. Keep the approved wording with the evidence so the next brochure revision can be checked efficiently.

Keep product identities stable

GS1’s product-management principles illustrate why relevant product changes need controlled identification. Apply the same discipline to brochure tables: a new profile or surface version should not silently retain old supporting claims. Record the model reference used in photographs, sample captions and quote templates. If the same marketing name covers several constructions, explain the distinction rather than allowing the reader to infer identical specifications.

Review translation for stronger meanings

Ask a reviewer to compare the meaning of technical claims, not merely spelling. Words such as resistant, waterproof, certified and guaranteed are not interchangeable. Ensure that an illustration remains an illustration in every version and that a proposed option is not translated as an available standard item. Preserve numbers, units and model references exactly unless a verified conversion is intentionally provided. Do not invent local approvals to make a translated brochure sound more persuasive.

Issue a controlled sales version

Record the brochure revision and retire outdated copies from active sales channels. Give staff a short list of claims that require a current model-specific response rather than a blanket answer. If local regulatory or advertising requirements apply, obtain appropriate local advice. The practical objective is a brochure that helps buyers ask informed questions, not one that hides uncertainty behind the strongest possible language.

Before you request a quotation

  • Inventory claims in copy, tables, badges and captions.
  • Match evidence to the exact offered model.
  • Check translated meaning and numerical consistency.
  • Retire outdated versions from active circulation.

Frequently asked questions

Can one test report support every panel in a catalogue?

Only if its documented scope genuinely covers them. Do not extend a result to unrelated models or assemblies without appropriate evidence.

Should every uncertain statement be replaced by a softer adjective?

Not necessarily. Sometimes the useful answer is to remove the claim and request specific information, rather than leaving an ambiguous promise.
