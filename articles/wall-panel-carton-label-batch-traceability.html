What should a wall panel carton label let a distributor trace?

A pallet arrives with several finishes, then the distributor splits it between a showroom, two dealers and a project site. Weeks later a customer asks which batch supplied one room. A product name alone may not answer that question. This guide proposes a simple receiving and dispatch record for PVC, WPC and PU panel buyers. It is a workflow to agree with suppliers, not a claim that every existing carton already carries these fields.

Short answer

The label and linked records should let you connect a carton to the exact product, supplier batch where available, purchase order and onward delivery. Keep the original identity when splitting or relabelling stock. A barcode by itself is not traceability.

Independent reference

GS1: traceability and supply-chain identification. General guidance, not a Luvie product certification.

Distinguish a model code from a batch and a logistics identifier

A model or SKU identifies what was ordered. A batch identifies a production grouping, while a pallet or shipment identifier describes a logistics unit. GS1’s traceability framework distinguishes identification and the information exchanged through the supply chain. For a small distributor, the practical question is whether records can follow the goods after handling. Do not invent a manufacturer batch when none is supplied. Assign an internal receipt reference if needed, clearly label it as internal and preserve the supplier’s original information beside it.

Agree a readable label and a matching receiving record

Before shipment, ask for a proposed label example and agree the fields relevant to the order: product reference, finish, pack quantity and unit, supplied batch identification and purchase-order link. Record dimensions only from the approved specification. Ask whether a quantity describes pieces, cartons or area; those units must not be silently exchanged. Test whether warehouse staff can read the label without opening every pack. A Spanish or Portuguese description can help local handling, but keep the original code unchanged. For Arabic records, preserve alphanumeric identifiers exactly instead of translating their characters.

Preserve the link when a pallet is split

When cartons move to a dealer or site, record which receipt or supplier batch was sent, how much and to which delivery reference. If stock is repacked, link the new pack identifier to its original cartons before discarding outer packaging. Do not combine unidentified leftovers under a new product label. Keep retained spare panels linked to the project where practical. A simple spreadsheet can be sufficient to start if the fields are consistent and staff update it at the actual movement, not from memory at month end.

Test the record with a realistic customer question

Choose one dispatched carton and ask the team to find its purchase order, receipt, product specification and destination. Then work forward from a selected receipt to the deliveries that used it. Missing links identify a process gap before a complaint occurs. Batch identity can help narrow a colour or damage investigation, but it does not prove that every piece in the batch is defective or that pieces from different batches will match. Keep appearance approval, condition photographs and any technical test evidence separate. Set access permissions and retain records under the company’s applicable policy.

Next step

Agree the label example and receiving fields before the next mixed order. Send selected product references and packing needs to discuss what information can be supplied, then test the record through one complete delivery.
