SPC versus glue-down LVT: what the Camaro specifications actually show

SPC is not automatically a higher-performance version of flexible LVT. A useful comparison keeps the manufacturer, named product and test method visible. This worked example compares Polyflor Camaro Rigid Core PUR, explicitly identified as Stone Polymer Composite, with flexible, adhered Camaro PUR. Quayside Oak 4139 and 2246 are named designs in the respective specifications, not interchangeable codes. The figures below come from the manufacturer’s linked specification sheets, both carrying V3 footers, checked on 23 November 2026. These are third-party examples, not Luvie specifications, stock offers or evidence that every SPC and LVT product behaves alike.

Short answer

The rigid example is 3.5 mm thicker and 4.97 kg/m² heavier, while both declare the same 0.30 mm wear layer and use classes 23, 31. The glued product declares a smaller residual-indentation value; the rigid construction declares greater laboratory impact-sound reduction. None of those differences establishes an overall winner. Choose the assembly that fits the actual height, substrate, movement, loading and acoustic brief.

Separate floor height, wear layer and delivery weight

Subtracting the declared gauges gives 5.5 − 2.0 = 3.5 mm. That is a product-thickness difference, not the difference between finished floor levels: adhesive, smoothing and other approved preparation belong in the build-up. The rigid sheet also identifies an integral 1 mm IXPE layer; do not add it again to the declared overall gauge. Equal wear layers do not make the complete constructions identical or predict equal service life. For a hypothetical 100 m² of product, declared net material mass is 857 kg versus 360 kg, a 497 kg difference. This excludes cartons, pallets, waste and installation materials; use actual packing data for freight quotations.

Read indentation and stability without creating a durability score

The two indentation entries use the same cited method, but they are declared bounds with an average-production-test footnote, not measurements of your proposed shipment. Do not turn ≤0.10 and <0.05 into a claim that one floor dents exactly twice as much. Obtain project-specific advice for furniture feet, concentrated loads or wheeled equipment. The dimensional-stability numbers match, yet the rigid sheet flags its value as an average whereas the glued sheet does not mark that row the same way. Neither entry removes movement requirements. Abrasion is less directly comparable: a cycle count under one method and Group T under another are not a common numerical scale.

Treat acoustic numbers as assembly evidence, not room promises

The declared impact-sound-reduction figures differ by 16 dB arithmetically. That does not mean an occupied room will sound 16 dB quieter, and it is not a percentage reduction. The rigid product includes an acoustic backing; the adhered product is a different construction. Although both sheets cite ISO 10140-3, this summary does not establish identical test assemblies, substrates or installation conditions. Request the underlying reports for an acoustic requirement and have the project specialist assess their relevance. Do not subtract these figures from a building’s existing test result or transfer them to another underlay, another brand or a proposed Luvie model.

Know when the SPC option should be rejected

The linked rigid plank instructions require a suitable hard, dry and flat base, exclude carpet or soft floorings as a base, and do not recommend continuously wet areas such as walk-in showers. They also require movement accommodation and separate floors at doorways between rooms. Therefore reject this floating option if the approved design cannot accommodate those conditions; a waterproof plank claim does not create a watertight room. A lower-profile glued solution may merit review where build-up is constrained, but it still needs its own substrate, adhesive and moisture assessment. Neither quoted class automatically approves a heavy-duty commercial application beyond the named product’s documented scope.

Before you request a quotation

  • Identify the exact product, design code and current specification revision.
  • Price substrate preparation, adhesive or floating details, trims and finished height separately.
  • Preserve test methods, inequality signs, footnotes and acoustic assembly questions.
  • Reject incompatible site conditions before ranking quotations by price.

Frequently asked questions

Does the thicker SPC example have a thicker wear layer?

No. Both named products declare 0.30 mm. Overall thickness measures a different aspect of construction and should not be used as a substitute for wear-layer information.

Can these figures be entered into a Luvie quotation?

Only as clearly identified comparison requirements, never as Luvie test results. Request current evidence for the exact Luvie model and check whether its methods and construction are comparable.

Next step
